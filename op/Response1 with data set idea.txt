Project Deep Dive & Dataset Collection (Issue #14)
1. Complete Understanding of the Project
1.1 The Core Problem

Traditional cybersecurity focuses on perimeter defense and intrusion detection. However, modern multi-stage attacks and ransomware inevitably breach preventive controls. Once a compromise occurs:

Traditional SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response) relies on static playbooks that cannot adapt to evolving adversary behavior.
Operators do not know what assets, telemetry sources, and backup snapshots remain trustworthy.
Restoring the newest backup often results in contaminated recovery (re-infecting clean environments with dormant backdoors or encrypted payloads).
Mean Time to Respond (MTTR) and recovery times escalate to days or weeks.
1.2 The Core Novel Contribution: Trust-Conditioned Recovery Planning (TCRP)

Your project does not simply string together LLMs with APIs; it introduces a mathematical decision mechanism: TCRP. TCRP dynamically evaluates the trustworthiness of candidate recovery points and affected assets, enforces policy gates, and optimizes service restoration in dependency order.

1.3 Insights from Your Repository's Research Papers
Hammar et al. (NDSS 2026): Demonstrates that fine-tuned 14B models with lookahead planning reduce response hallucination. Gap addressed by your project: Their model assumes backups are inherently trusted; your project adds the recovery trust score layer.
Tellache et al. (IEEE GLOBECOM 2025): RAG-based CTI retrieval for SIEM alerts using VirusTotal and threat reports. Relevance: Direct blueprint for your CTI and Triage Agent alert-enrichment pipeline.
Schlette et al. (IEEE S&P 2024): Empirical study of 1,217 SOAR playbooks. Relevance: 96.9% of playbook actions are automated, but they lack dynamic contextual trust evaluation and backup validation.
NIST SP 800-184: Cybersecurity event recovery lifecycle, backup integrity checks, and dependency maps. Relevance: Foundational standard for defining your recovery metrics and stages.
Kizhakel & Pande (Discover AI 2026 - CIPHER-A): Counterfactual validation before action execution and adversarial lookahead using MITRE ATT&CK. Relevance: Direct reference for the Counterfactual Validation Engine and Attacker Simulation Agent.
Zhang et al. (RESS 2026): Multi-source evidence fusion using Dempster-Shafer theory and utility theory to resolve conflicting evidence. Relevance: Informs conflict resolution when telemetry sources may have been tampered with by the adversary.

3. Dataset Collection & Evaluation (Work Done for Issue #14)
3.1 Public vs. Lab-Generated Datasets

Public Datasets (Macro & Micro Intrusion Telemetry):

Network Traffic: CIC-IDS-2017, CSE-CIC-IDS-2018, UNSW-NB15, NSL-KDD.
Malware & Memory Forensics: CIC-MalMem-2022 (Ransomware: Conti, MAZE, Pysa), EMBER 2018 (1.1M PE features).
Enterprise Security Logs: LANL Cyber (1.64B events across 58 days), Splunk BOTS (v1–v3).
Threat Intelligence & APT: MITRE ATT&CK STIX 2.1 (26,699 objects), DARPA OpTC (500+ host provenance graphs), OTRF/Mordor, CTU-13.

Lab-Generated Datasets (Recovery & Trust Specific):

Public datasets provide attack logs, but zero public datasets provide candidate recovery points with ground-truth backup contamination status.
Created full blueprints, simulation scripts, and synthetic benchmark generators in datasets/lab-generated/:
Ransomware Simulation: Safe canary encryption script (simulate_ransomware_activity.py) that outputs file mutation and entropy logs.
Backup Trust Validation: Benchmark dataset generator (generate_synthetic_rts_eval.py) that generated synthetic_rts_benchmark.csv with 500 candidate recovery points across 6 operational scenarios.
Multi-Stage APT & Blast Radius: Enterprise 4-tier service dependency DAG (service_dependency_dag.json) modeling topological restoration constraints.
