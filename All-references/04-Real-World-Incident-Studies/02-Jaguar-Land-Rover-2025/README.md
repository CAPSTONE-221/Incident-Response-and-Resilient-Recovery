# Jaguar Land Rover Cyber Incident (2025)

## Purpose of this case study
This folder records the Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) cyber incident as a second real-world example of major operational disruption. The sources below were identified in the project review references.

## Sources

1. **BleepingComputer** — “Jaguar Land Rover cyberattack cost the company over $220 million” (Bill Toulas, 15 November 2025).  
   https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/jaguar-land-rover-cyberattack-cost-the-company-over-220-million/

2. **Cyber Monitoring Centre** — “Cyber Monitoring Centre Statement on the Jaguar Land Rover Cyber Incident – October 2025” (22 October 2025).  
   https://cybermonitoringcentre.com/2025/10/22/cyber-monitoring-centre-statement-on-the-jaguar-land-rover-cyber-incident-october-2025/

3. **Botcrawl** — “Jaguar Land Rover Data Breach Causes £196 Million in Losses and Halts Global Production” (Sean Doyle, 15 November 2025).  
   https://botcrawl.com/jaguar-land-rover-data-breach/

## Source quality
Prioritise the Cyber Monitoring Centre statement for its incident assessment and BleepingComputer for security reporting. Treat Botcrawl as a secondary source and corroborate any figures or technical claims before relying on them.

## Relevance to the project
The case can support discussion of:
- cyber incidents that disrupt manufacturing and supply-chain operations;
- recovery as restoration of an operational system rather than simply restoration of individual servers;
- service and process dependencies;
- the need to validate systems before returning them to production;
- the operational and economic consequences of extended downtime.

## Analysis cautions
- Clearly distinguish company-reported losses, analyst estimates, and wider economic-impact estimates.
- Do not assume the incident's exact initial access method or attacker identity unless the cited source explicitly supports it.
- Do not present a news report as a peer-reviewed technical analysis.
- Do not reproduce full article text.
